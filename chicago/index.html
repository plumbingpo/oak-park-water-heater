Keeping Your Chicago Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year in hard-water areas.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Chicago

Whatever type of water heater your Chicago home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units burn out elements and thermostats, especially when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
